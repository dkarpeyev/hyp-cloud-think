# Quantum mechanics as intro/elim rules

**Short answer:** Yes, but in pieces. Nobody has a single, settled "natural deduction for QM" the way intuitionistic logic stands to the simply typed λ-calculus. There are several research programmes, and each captures a different part of the theory:

- the structure of quantum *states and processes*: linear logic, dagger-compact categories, quantum λ-calculi
- *superposition and measurement* as logical connectives: Díaz-Caro and Dowek
- the *logic of propositions about* quantum systems: quantum logic sequent calculi, topos approaches
- a *full dependent type theory* aiming at quantum physics: linear homotopy type theory

(Nothing on this turned up in your THINK folder, so everything below comes from my own knowledge of the literature. Treat the citations as pointers to check, not verified bibliography entries.)

---

## 1. The core idea: linearity is no-cloning

The basic observation behind nearly all of these is that the **structural rules** are where QM differs from classical logic:

| Classical rule | Quantum reading |
|---|---|
| Contraction (Γ, A, A ⊢ B ⟹ Γ, A ⊢ B) | cloning, which the no-cloning theorem forbids |
| Weakening (Γ ⊢ B ⟹ Γ, A ⊢ B) | discarding, which is allowed in mixed-state/CPM settings but not in pure/unitary ones |

So you start from **linear logic** (Girard 1987). Then:

- **⊗-intro** builds a composite system: from Γ ⊢ a : A and Δ ⊢ b : B, derive Γ, Δ ⊢ a ⊗ b : A ⊗ B.
- **⊗-elim** (`let x ⊗ y = t in u`) uses both parts together. Entangled states are closed terms of type A ⊗ B that are not themselves of the form a ⊗ b. This works semantically because the tensor in **FdHilb** is not a cartesian product.
- **⊸-intro/elim** gives higher-order quantum processes. The compact-closed structure (A ⊸ B ≅ A* ⊗ B) is the categorical form of map–state duality (Choi–Jamiołkowski).

Classical data comes back in through the **! modality** (or a linear/non-linear adjunction): `!A` may be copied and deleted.

## 2. Quantum λ-calculi (Curry–Howard for quantum computation)

These are the most developed "intro/elim" formalisms. Classical control is handled by ! or by LNL.

- **Selinger (2004)**, "Towards a quantum programming language", *MSCS* 14(4). Quantum flowcharts and the first denotational semantics via superoperators.
- **Selinger & Valiron (2006)**, "A lambda calculus for quantum computation with classical control", *MSCS* 16(3). The canonical reference. It uses affine intuitionistic linear types with `!`, and `meas : qbit ⊸ !bit` is the elimination form that destroys the quantum resource.
- **van Tonder (2004)**, "A lambda calculus for quantum computation", *SIAM J. Comput.* 33(5).
- **Altenkirch & Grattage (2005)**, "A functional quantum programming language" (QML), LICS. Has a quantum conditional `if°` whose typing rule requires an *orthogonality judgment* on its branches. This is one of the first places a QM-specific side condition appears inside an elim rule.
- **Benton (1994)**, "A mixed linear and non-linear logic" (LNL). Not quantum itself, but it is the backbone of later systems.
- **Pagani, Selinger & Valiron (2014)**, "Applying quantitative semantics to higher-order quantum computing", POPL. A fully abstract-style model for the higher-order case.
- **Paykin, Rand & Zdancewic (2017)**, "QWIRE: a core language for quantum circuits", POPL. Formalized in Coq.
- **Ross (2015)**, PhD thesis on Proto-Quipper. **Rios & Selinger (2017)**, "A categorical model for a quantum circuit description language", QPL.
- **Fu, Kishida & Selinger (2020)**, "Linear dependent type theory for quantum programming languages", LICS. This is *dependent* linear types, Proto-Quipper-D.
- **Zorzi (2016)**, "On quantum lambda calculi: a foundational perspective", *MSCS* 26(7). A good survey to start from.
- **Dal Lago, Masini & Zorzi (2009)**, "On a measurement-free quantum lambda calculus with classical control", *MSCS* 19(2).

## 3. Superposition and measurement as connectives (closest to your literal question)

This line asks directly: *what are the intro and elim rules for superposition?*

- **Díaz-Caro & Dowek**, "A new connective in natural deduction, and its application to quantum computing". It appeared at ICTAC 2021, with an extended version in *Theoretical Computer Science* (around 2023). It introduces a connective **⊙ ("sup")**:
  - **⊙-intro** looks like ∧-intro: from proofs of A and B, form a proof of A ⊙ B, a superposition α·a + β·b.
  - **⊙-elim** looks like ∨-elim/projection: you get *either* A *or* B, non-deterministically. This is measurement.
  - So the connective has the introduction rule of conjunction and the elimination rule of disjunction. That mismatch is the logical signature of superposition followed by collapse. Cut elimination corresponds to quantum algorithms. They encode Deutsch's algorithm, teleportation, etc.
- **Arrighi & Dowek (2017)**, "Lineal: a linear-algebraic λ-calculus", *LMCS* 13(1). Terms form a vector space, with superposition at the term level.
- **Arrighi, Díaz-Caro & Valiron (2017)**, "The vectorial λ-calculus", *Information & Computation* 254.
- **Díaz-Caro, Guillermo, Miquel & Valiron (2019)**, "Realizability in the unitary sphere", LICS. The types characterize exactly the unitary operators, giving *quantum control* rather than classical control.
- **Díaz-Caro & Malherbe (2022)**, "Quantum control in the unitary sphere: Lambda-S1 and its categorical model", *LMCS*.
- **Díaz-Caro & Dowek (2022)**, "Linear lambda-calculus is linear", FSCD. Proofs in linear logic denote linear maps.
- **Sabry, Valiron & Vizzotto (2018)**, "From symmetric pattern-matching to quantum control", FoSSaCS.
- **Chardonnet, Saurin & Valiron (2023)**, "A Curry–Howard correspondence for linear, reversible computation", CSL. Uses μMALL and isos, aiming at unitary (reversible) quantum control.

## 4. Categorical quantum mechanics and its proof theory

This programme gives the semantics that the type theories above are interpreted in. It also has its own sequent and proof-net calculi.

- **Abramsky & Coecke (2004)**, "A categorical semantics of quantum protocols", LICS. QM is axiomatized as a dagger-compact category with biproducts, and teleportation becomes a diagrammatic proof.
- **Abramsky & Duncan (2006)**, "A categorical quantum logic", *MSCS* 16(3). An explicit **sequent/proof-net calculus** for CQM, with cut elimination. **Duncan (2006)**, DPhil thesis "Types for quantum computing", Oxford.
- **Selinger (2007)**, "Dagger compact closed categories and completely positive maps", QPL. This is the CPM construction, which takes you from pure to mixed states, where weakening becomes legitimate.
- **Coecke & Duncan (2011)**, "Interacting quantum observables: categorical algebra and diagrammatics", *New J. Phys.* 13. The ZX-calculus, an *equational* rather than intro/elim presentation, later proved complete (Jeandel–Perdrix–Vilmart, Ng–Wang, 2018).
- **Cockett, Comfort & Srinivasan (2021)**, "Dagger linear logic for categorical quantum mechanics", *LMCS* 17(4). Puts the dagger into linear logic proper.
- **Staton (2015)**, "Algebraic effects, linearity, and quantum programming languages", POPL. An equational theory of qubits as an algebraic effect.
- Textbooks: **Coecke & Kissinger**, *Picturing Quantum Processes* (CUP 2017). **Heunen & Vicary**, *Categories for Quantum Theory* (OUP 2019).
- Earlier: **Yetter (1990)**, "Quantales and (noncommutative) linear logic", *JSL*. **Pratt (1992)**, "Linear logic for generalized quantum mechanics", on Chu spaces.

## 5. Dependent type theory for QM: linear homotopy type theory

This is the most ambitious programme: a *full foundational* type theory in which quantum physics is internal.

- **Schreiber (2014)**, "Quantization via linear homotopy types", arXiv:1402.7041. Quantization is pull–push along linear (spectral, module-valued) dependent types.
- **Schreiber & Shulman (2014)**, "Quantum gauge field theory in cohesive homotopy type theory", QPL.
- **Riley (2022)**, PhD thesis "A linear dependent type theory with identity types" (Wesleyan). This is LHoTT, with intro/elim rules for the ⊗ and ⊸ of a linear dependent type theory compatible with HoTT.
- **Sati & Schreiber (2023)**, "The quantum monadology", arXiv:2310.15735. Measurement and quantum effects become (co)monads on linear types indexed over classical types. Measuring a B-indexed system is the counit/unit of a Frobenius-type monad ◯_B. **Sati & Schreiber**, "Entanglement of sections" (2023), on parameterized quantum information.
- **Myers, Riley, Sati & Schreiber**, follow-up work on "LHoTT" as a quantum programming language. Check arXiv; the 2023–24 titles vary.

## 6. Logic *about* quantum systems (quantum logic proper)

Here the propositions are "the observable has a value in Δ", not states.

- **Birkhoff & von Neumann (1936)**, "The logic of quantum mechanics", *Ann. Math.* 37. Orthomodular lattices. Distributivity fails, so ∨-elim breaks: you cannot reason by cases.
- **Nishimura (1980, 1994)**, "Proof theory for minimal quantum logic I/II", *Int. J. Theor. Phys.* Sequent calculi, with cut-elimination problems.
- **Faggian & Sambin (1998)**, "From basic logic to quantum logics with cut-elimination", *IJTP* 37. **Sambin, Battilotti & Faggian (2000)**, "Basic logic: reflection, symmetry, visibility", *JSL* 65. These are the most careful attempts at *harmonious* intro/elim rules for quantum logic, where the rules are obtained by "reflection" from definitional equations.
- **Dalla Chiara, Giuntini & Greechie (2004)**, *Reasoning in Quantum Theory* (Kluwer). Standard monograph.
- **Baltag & Smets**, "LQP: the dynamic logic of quantum information" (*MSCS* 2006) and "Quantum logic as a dynamic logic" (*Synthese* 2011). Measurement is a dynamic modality, closer to PDL than to natural deduction.
- **Topos approach:** **Döring & Isham (2008)**, "A topos foundation for theories of physics", *J. Math. Phys.* 49. **Heunen, Landsman & Spitters (2009)**, "A topos for algebraic quantum theory", *CMP* 291. Propositions about a quantum system form an *intuitionistic* internal logic. You get ordinary natural deduction back, interpreted over the poset of classical contexts (commutative subalgebras). Kochen–Specker becomes the nonexistence of a global section.

## 7. Program logics and machine formalization (adjacent)

- **Ying (2011)**, "Floyd–Hoare logic for quantum programs", *TOPLAS* 33(6). **Unruh (2019)**, "Quantum relational Hoare logic", POPL. **Zhou, Barthe, Hsu, Ying & Yu (2021)**, "A quantum interpretation of bunched logic & quantum separation logic", LICS.
- Proof assistants: QWIRE and SQIR (Hietala et al., POPL 2021) in Coq; **CoqQ** (Zhou et al., POPL 2023); **Isabelle Marries Dirac** (Bordg, Lachnitt & He, 2021). These formalize Hilbert-space QM *inside* an ordinary type theory, not *as* one.

## Summary

| Quantum feature | Logical counterpart |
|---|---|
| no-cloning / no-deleting | no contraction / no weakening (linear logic) |
| composite systems, entanglement | ⊗, non-cartesian |
| map–state duality | compact closure, A ⊸ B ≅ A* ⊗ B |
| superposition then collapse | ⊙: ∧-like intro, ∨-like elim (Díaz-Caro–Dowek) |
| measurement | elim into `!bit`, or a (co)monad indexed by classical outcomes (Sati–Schreiber) |
| unitarity / reversibility | isos, μMALL, the "unitary sphere" realizability |
| contextuality (Kochen–Specker) | failure of distributivity (quantum logic), or no global section (topos) |

**What's still open:** No current system has a harmonious intro/elim presentation that captures *all* of finite-dimensional QM (amplitudes, unitarity, measurement and the Born rule) with a clean cut-elimination theorem and a completeness result for FdHilb. The Born rule's probabilities are usually added through the semantics, such as CPM or density matrices, not derived from the rules.

**Where to start:**
- For the proof theory: Díaz-Caro–Dowek on ⊙.
- For type theory with classical control: Selinger–Valiron.
- For dependent types: Riley's thesis plus Sati–Schreiber.

# Summary: Díaz-Caro & Dowek, "A new connective in natural deduction, and its application to quantum computing"

## The idea

The paper adds a new binary connective **⊙ ("sup", for superposition)** to natural deduction. It is built by mixing the rules of existing connectives:

- **Introduction:** the rule of **conjunction**. To prove A ⊙ B you need a proof of A *and* a proof of B.
- **Elimination:** the rule of **disjunction**. From A ⊙ B you may continue with *either* A *or* B, but not both.

The proof term for ⊙-intro is a pair `[t, u]`, and the elimination is a case construct `δ⊙`:

```
 Γ ⊢ t : A    Γ ⊢ u : B                 Γ ⊢ t : A ⊙ B    Γ, x:A ⊢ v : C    Γ, y:B ⊢ w : C
───────────────────────── ⊙i            ───────────────────────────────────────────────── ⊙e
   Γ ⊢ [t, u] : A ⊙ B                              Γ ⊢ δ⊙(t, x.v, y.w) : C
```

Cut reduction is **non-deterministic**:

```
δ⊙([t, u], x.v, y.w)  ⟶  v[t/x]
δ⊙([t, u], x.v, y.w)  ⟶  w[u/y]
```

A proof of A ⊙ B carries both components, but any use of it keeps only one. That is the logical shape of a superposition followed by a measurement.

## Logical properties

- **Comparison with "tonk".** Prior's 1960 connective tonk has ∨-intro and ∧-elim. That combination makes everything provable. ⊙ is the reverse combination (∧-intro, ∨-elim), and it is **consistent**.
- **Provability.** A ⊙ B is equivalent to A ∧ B as far as provability goes. The difference is in the **proofs**: you can't project a component out deterministically, you can only branch on it. So ⊙ is invisible at the level of "what is provable" and only shows up at the level of proof terms. That is why the paper's contribution is really to the proof theory (Curry–Howard), not to logic as a consequence relation.
- **Meta-theory of the proof language** (as I recall it; this is where to check the paper):
  - subject reduction
  - strong normalization, proved by reducibility
  - an introduction property: closed normal proofs end in an introduction rule

## Application to quantum computing

The authors then add **scalars**, giving a calculus they call something like ⊙^ℂ:

- **Qubits.** Write `*` for the trivial proof of ⊤. A qubit α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ is a proof `[α.*, β.*]` of **⊤ ⊙ ⊤**.
- **Measurement.** Eliminating ⊙ is measurement. Reduction becomes **probabilistic**: it takes the first branch with probability |α|² / (|α|² + |β|²) and the second with the complementary probability. Non-determinism becomes the Born rule.
- **Gates.** Gates such as Hadamard are proofs of (⊤ ⊙ ⊤) ⇒ (⊤ ⊙ ⊤) that act on the pair componentwise, as linear maps.
- **Multiple qubits.** These are built by combining ⊙ with ∧ / tensor-like structure.
- **Algorithms.** They encode standard examples. Deutsch's algorithm is the main one; I believe the extended version adds more, such as teleportation or Deutsch–Jozsa, but I'm unsure exactly which.

## Limitations

- **No-cloning isn't enforced.** The base calculus is *not linear*: contexts can be shared, so a qubit can be duplicated. This is acknowledged as a gap.
- **Later work fixes this.** The follow-ups move to linear settings: Díaz-Caro & Dowek, "Linear lambda-calculus is linear" (FSCD 2022), and their linear proof language for second-order intuitionistic linear logic with sums and scalars.
- **Measurement is built in.** It is *the* elimination rule, not a separate primitive. So you can't keep a superposition and use it without collapsing it, except through the term-level linear operations. Systems with quantum control (Lambda-S, the "unitary sphere" work) treat this differently.

## Why it matters for your question

This is the clearest answer to "QM via intro/elim rules". Superposition gets a connective of its own, measurement *is* cut elimination, and the Born rule is the reduction strategy. What it doesn't give you is unitarity or linearity at the level of the logic. Those come from the later linear versions and from the Lambda-S / unitary-sphere line.

# Is there a formalization of QBism?

**Short answer:** Partly. QBism has a real mathematical formalization: the **SIC / Urgleichung / qplex** programme of Fuchs, Appleby, Stacey, DeBrota and others. Its aim is to rewrite quantum theory entirely in terms of an agent's probabilities and then reconstruct Hilbert space from conditions on those probabilities. I know of no formalization of QBism as a logic or type theory with intro/elim rules, and none in a proof assistant like Lean or Coq. The nearest logical work is on multi-agent epistemic reasoning, prompted by Wigner's friend and Frauchiger–Renner.

## 1. The core formalization: QM as a modified probability calculus

**Step 1: a reference measurement.** Pick a SIC-POVM in dimension *d*. It has d² outcomes, with effects E_i = (1/d)Π_i, where the Π_i are rank-one projectors and tr(Π_iΠ_j) = 1/(d+1) for i ≠ j. Because it is *informationally complete*, any state ρ is fixed by its outcome probabilities p(i) = tr(ρE_i). **The quantum state is just a probability vector** p ∈ Δ_{d²}. That is QBism's tenet 2 made literal.

**Step 2: the Born rule as an "Urgleichung" (primal equation).** Compare two scenarios:
- you do the SIC and then some measurement {F_j}
- you do {F_j} directly

Classical coherence (the law of total probability) would give q(j) = Σ_i p(i) r(j|i). The Born rule instead says:

$$q(j) \;=\; \sum_i \Big[(d+1)\,p(i) - \tfrac{1}{d}\Big]\, r(j\mid i)$$

QBism's claim is that **this is the whole content of quantum theory beyond probability theory.** It is an extra normative constraint that links one agent's probabilities for counterfactual experiments. The theory doesn't describe what the world is; it is an addition to Bayesian coherence.

- Fuchs & Schack, "Quantum-Bayesian coherence", *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **85**, 1693 (2013), arXiv:0906.2187. **The central reference.**
- DeBrota, Fuchs, Pienaar & Stacey, "Born's rule as a quantum extension of Bayesian coherence", *Phys. Rev. A* **104**, 022207 (2021), arXiv:2012.14397. Generalizes beyond SICs to any informationally complete reference measurement, and shows SICs are optimal (they minimize the departure from the classical law).
- DeBrota, Fuchs & Stacey, "Symmetric informationally complete measurements identify the irreducible difference between classical and quantum systems", *Phys. Rev. Research* **2**, 013074 (2020), arXiv:1805.08721.

## 2. Reconstruction: the qplex

The second step is to drop Hilbert space and **axiomatize the set of allowed probability vectors directly**:

- Appleby, Fuchs, Stacey & Zhu, "Introducing the qplex: a novel arena for quantum theory", *Eur. Phys. J. D* **71**, 197 (2017), arXiv:1612.03234.

A **qplex** is a maximal set of vectors p ∈ Δ_{d²} that is consistent with the Urgleichung and satisfies the *fundamental inequalities*:

$$\frac{1}{d(d+1)} \;\le\; \langle p, q\rangle \;\le\; \frac{2}{d(d+1)}$$

The main theorem says that **a qplex is isomorphic to quantum state space** (via a SIC) exactly when its symmetry group contains a subgroup of the right kind. In particular, Hilbert-space quantum theory comes out once you impose enough symmetry. There is a catch: the reconstruction depends on **SICs existing in every dimension (Zauner's conjecture)**. That is still open, though there is strong numerical and algebraic evidence, including a surprising link to Hilbert's 12th problem (Appleby, Flammia, McConnell & Yard, 2016–17). For an overview see Fuchs, Hoang & Stacey, "The SIC question: history and state of play", *Axioms* **6**, 21 (2017).

Background and motivation:
- Fuchs & Stacey, "QBism: quantum theory as a hero's handbook" (2019), arXiv:1612.07308. The most complete exposition that includes the formalism.
- Stacey, "Quantum theory as symmetry broken by vitality" (2019), arXiv:1907.02432.

## 3. Bayesian foundations: Dutch books and de Finetti

These results make "quantum states are personal probability assignments" mathematically respectable:

- Caves, Fuchs & Schack, "Quantum probabilities as Bayesian probabilities", *Phys. Rev. A* **65**, 022305 (2002). Gleason-type results from a Dutch-book argument.
- Caves, Fuchs & Schack, "Unknown quantum states: the quantum de Finetti representation", *J. Math. Phys.* **43**, 4537 (2002). An "unknown state" ∫ρ^{⊗n} dμ(ρ) is reinterpreted as an exchangeable personal belief. This is how QBism handles state tomography without objective states.
- Fuchs, Schack & Scudo, "De Finetti representation theorem for quantum process tomography", *Phys. Rev. A* **69**, 062305 (2004).
- Pitowsky, "Betting on the outcomes of measurements: a Bayesian theory of quantum probability", *Stud. Hist. Phil. Mod. Phys.* **34** (2003). Not QBist, but closely related: quantum probability as rational betting on a non-Boolean event structure.

## 4. Logic-level work (the closest thing to an intro/elim treatment)

Nobody has given QBism itself a proof theory. What exists formalizes **multi-agent quantum reasoning**, which is where QBism's "who assigns this state?" makes a difference:

- Frauchiger & Renner, "Quantum theory cannot consistently describe the use of itself", *Nat. Commun.* **9**, 3711 (2018). It states explicit reasoning rules (the assumptions Q, C, S) that agents use.
- Nurgalieva & del Rio, "Inadequacy of modal logic in quantum settings", QPL 2018 (EPTCS 287). Recasts Frauchiger–Renner in **epistemic modal logic** (Kripke frames, knowledge operators K_A) and pins down which axiom fails: distribution of knowledge across agents.
- Vilasini, Nurgalieva & del Rio, "Multi-agent paradoxes beyond quantum theory", *New J. Phys.* **21**, 113028 (2019). Extends this to general probabilistic theories.
- DeBrota, Fuchs & Schack, "Respecting one's fellow: QBism's analysis of Wigner's friend", *Found. Phys.* **50** (2020). The QBist reply: agent-indexed probabilities block the inference step that Frauchiger–Renner need.
- Baltag & Smets, the LQP / dynamic-epistemic quantum logic work mentioned earlier. It isn't QBist, but it already has agents, belief update and measurement as actions, which makes it the most natural formal home for QBist ideas.

## 5. What's missing, and how it connects to your earlier question

| Formalization | Status |
|---|---|
| Probability-calculus form of QM (SIC, Urgleichung) | Done (Fuchs–Schack 2013, DeBrota et al. 2021) |
| Reconstruction of state space from probabilistic axioms | Partial: the qplex theorem, which assumes SICs exist |
| Dutch-book / de Finetti foundations | Done for states and processes |
| Multi-agent modal / epistemic logic | Exists for Wigner's friend and Frauchiger–Renner, not QBism-specific |
| Natural deduction / type theory for QBism | **Nothing I know of** |
| Proof-assistant formalization | **Nothing I know of** |

A QBist type theory would look quite different from the Selinger–Valiron or Díaz-Caro–Dowek systems. Those treat a qubit as a *resource* or a *proof term*. For QBism, the basic judgment would be **agent-indexed** (something like "agent A has credence p over their experience of action E"). Measurement would be a *dynamic* rule for acting and updating, not an elimination that destroys a resource. The Urgleichung would be a *coherence constraint* between judgments, not a reduction rule. That suggests something like a probabilistic dynamic-epistemic logic with the Urgleichung as an axiom, or a graded/indexed modal type theory. As far as I know, this is an open niche.

## Díaz-Caro & Dowek, "A new connective in natural deduction, and its application to quantum computing"
ICTAC 2021 (LNCS 12819), arXiv:2012.08994. Extended version in *Theoretical Computer Science* (2023). I'm fairly but not fully sure of these details.

**1. A new connective, ⊙ ("sup").**
- Its **introduction** rule is ∧-intro: from proofs t : A and u : B, form `[t, u] : A ⊙ B`.
- Its **elimination** rule is ∨-elim: `δ⊙(t, x.v, y.w) : C`, given branches for A and for B.

**2. Cut reduction is non-deterministic.** `δ⊙([t,u], x.v, y.w)` reduces to *either* `v[t/x]` *or* `w[u/y]`. A proof holds both components, but using it keeps only one. That is superposition followed by collapse.

**3. Logical status.**
- It is the reverse of Prior's "tonk" (∨-intro with ∧-elim), and unlike tonk it is consistent.
- A ⊙ B and A ∧ B prove the same things, but their *proofs* differ. So the contribution is to proof theory (Curry–Howard), not to what is provable.
- The proof language has subject reduction, strong normalization and the introduction property.

**4. Quantum reading.**
- Adding scalars, a qubit α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ is a proof `[α.*, β.*] : ⊤ ⊙ ⊤`.
- ⊙-elimination is **measurement**. Reduction is **probabilistic**, with Born weights |α|²/(|α|² + |β|²).
- Gates are proofs of (⊤⊙⊤) ⇒ (⊤⊙⊤). They encode Deutsch's algorithm, and I think the TCS version adds more examples.

**5. Limitations.**
- The calculus isn't linear, so it doesn't enforce no-cloning. Later linear work ("Linear λ-calculus is linear", FSCD 2022) addresses this.
- The Born weights are built into the reduction rule, not derived.
- Measurement is the only way to eliminate ⊙.
